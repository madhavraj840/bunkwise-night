Lux Auto Brightness alternative: what to use now¶

By Madhav Raj, who builds Screen & Notification Dimmer · Updated 25 November 2026 · 2 min read

Short answer

When I checked on 25 November 2026, Lux Auto Brightness's old Google Play address (com.vito.lux) returned "Not Found". If you used Lux to go below minimum brightness, any overlay dimmer such as Screen & Notification Dimmer does that. For automatic adjustment, Android's own adaptive brightness handles the room, and Screen & Notification Dimmer's Auto-Dim handles bright content on screen. For warm colours, use Night Light or a colour filter.

A note on bias

I make Screen & Notification Dimmer, so read this knowing that. Every fact about the other app comes from its own Google Play listing, checked on 25 November 2026. I have not claimed anything the listing does not say. If something is out of date, email support@bunkwise.in and I will correct it.

What happened to Lux?¶

Lux was a well-known brightness app for Android that took over from the system auto-brightness, learned from your adjustments and could dim below the minimum. I can't confirm why it is gone, only that its listing no longer loads on Google Play. If you still have an old copy installed, it may keep working, but an app that no longer gets updates can break on newer Android versions, which change overlay and background rules regularly.

Replacing each thing Lux did¶

What you used Lux forWhat to use now
Going darker than minimum brightnessAn overlay dimmer: Screen & Notification Dimmer, Penumbra or Night screen
Adjusting to the room automaticallyAndroid's Adaptive brightness (Settings > Display). It learns from your slider changes too
Avoiding bright flashes from white pagesScreen & Notification Dimmer Auto-Dim, which follows on-screen content (Android 11+)
Night-time profile at fixed hoursScreen & Notification Dimmer free bedtime schedule
Warm colour at nightAndroid's Night Light, or a red or amber filter
Per-app brightnessScreen & Notification Dimmer per-app levels

Why I'd pair two tools rather than one¶

Lux mixed two jobs: reading the light sensor and dimming the screen. Since Android 9, the system's adaptive brightness learns from you, so it now does the sensor job well. What it still can't do is go below the hardware minimum, or react when the content on screen suddenly turns white. Those are the gaps a dimmer fills. Let Android set the backlight, and let the dimmer handle the last stretch in the dark.
