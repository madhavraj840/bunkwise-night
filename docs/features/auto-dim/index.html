Auto-Dim: a dimmer that reacts to what is on screen¶

By Madhav Raj, who builds Screen & Notification Dimmer · Updated 25 November 2026 · 3 min read

Short answer

Auto-Dim changes the dimming level by itself based on how bright the content on your screen is. When a white page or a bright reel appears, it dims more. When you are back on a dark page, it eases off. It needs Android 11 or newer and the optional Accessibility service. It is a Pro feature, still marked beta, and one ad unlocks it for 12 hours.

At night the problem is often not the average brightness, it is the jumps. You are scrolling a dark feed at a comfortable level, then the next video opens on a white background and it feels like a camera flash. People call it a "flashbang". A fixed dimming level cannot help, because the level that is right for the dark feed is far too bright for the white page.

How Auto-Dim works¶

While dimming and Auto-Dim are both on, the app takes a screenshot through Android's Accessibility screenshot API several times a second. It shrinks each one to a tiny low-resolution grid and works out one number: how bright the content is. Bright content raises the dimming, dark content lowers it. The image is processed in memory and thrown away immediately. It is never saved, never written to storage and never sent anywhere.

The readout in the app shows what it sees, for example "Following the screen · content is 72% bright now".

Settings you can adjust¶

SettingWhat it changes
StrengthHow far the level moves between dark and bright content
Reaction speed: Gentle, Medium or FastHow quickly it responds. Gentle avoids flicker during fast scrolling; Fast catches sudden flashes sooner

How it differs from auto-brightness¶

Android's adaptive brightness and apps such as Lux or Super Dim's automatic mode respond to the room, using the light sensor. In a dark bedroom the room barely changes, so the sensor has nothing to react to. Auto-Dim responds to the content, which is what actually changes when a bright page opens. The two work well together: let the system handle the room, and let Auto-Dim handle the screen.

Limits, honestly¶

  • Android 11 or newer only. The screenshot API it relies on does not exist before Android 11.
  • Battery. It samples many times a second, so it uses more battery than plain dimming. The app says this up front, and how much depends on the phone.
  • Protected content. Some apps (banking apps and DRM video) block screenshots. Android may not hand over a usable screenshot there, so Auto-Dim may not react inside those apps.
  • Beta. I am still tuning it on different phones. If it misbehaves on yours, write to support@bunkwise.in with your phone model.

How to turn it on¶

  1. Go to the Automate tab and switch on Auto-Dim. Without Pro, tap Watch an ad for a 12-hour unlock.
  2. Allow the "Screen & Notification Dimmer - better dimming" Accessibility service when asked.
  3. Set Strength and Reaction speed, then open a mixed app such as a browser to see it respond.

You can also toggle it with the AUTO button on the notification or under the edge slider.

Frequently asked questions¶

Is there an app that dims bright white pages automatically?¶

Yes. Auto-Dim in Screen & Notification Dimmer raises the dimming when the content on screen turns bright and lowers it when the content is dark. It needs Android 11 or newer.

Does Auto-Dim record my screen?¶

No. It reduces each screenshot to a single brightness number in memory and discards the image immediately. Nothing is saved or sent.

Why does Auto-Dim not react in my banking app?¶

Apps that block screenshots may not give Android's screenshot API a usable image, so Auto-Dim may not react there. Give those apps a fixed per-app level instead.
