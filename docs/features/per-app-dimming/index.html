Per-app levels: each app keeps its own darkness¶

By Madhav Raj, who builds Screen & Notification Dimmer · Updated 25 November 2026 · 2 min read

Short answer

Per-app profiles let Screen & Notification Dimmer switch the dimming level automatically when you open a particular app. For example, YouTube at 70, your reading app at 30 and Maps at 0, while everything else stays at your usual level. It uses the optional Accessibility service only to know which app is in front. It is a Pro feature, and one ad unlocks it for 12 hours.

One level rarely fits everything. Video apps in a dark room want a lot of dimming. A white e-book page wants some. Maps in the car at night may need none at all, or you won't read the street names. Without per-app levels you end up moving the slider every time you switch apps.

How to set it up¶

  1. Go to Automate > Per-app profiles and switch it on (or Watch an ad for 12 hours).
  2. Tap Choose an app, search for it, and set the level it should dim to.
  3. Allow the "Night mode - better dimming" Accessibility service if you haven't already. It is the same permission the volume-button escape uses, so there is nothing extra to grant.

The page then shows a summary like "3 apps set · everything else stays at 40%".

What it can see¶

The service only notes the package name of the app in front, such as com.google.android.youtube, and only in memory. It never sees what is on the screen or what you type, and it keeps no history of the apps you used.

Frequently asked questions¶

Can I set different screen brightness for different apps on Android?¶

Android itself does not offer this, but Screen & Notification Dimmer's per-app profiles set a dimming level for each app and switch automatically when you open it.

Does per-app dimming read my screen?¶

No. It only knows the name of the app in front, in memory. It does not see screen content or keystrokes.
