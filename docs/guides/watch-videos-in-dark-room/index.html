Watching videos and reels in a dark room¶

By Madhav Raj, who builds Screen & Notification Dimmer · Updated 25 November 2026 · 2 min read

Short answer

Video is the hardest thing to dim at night, because brightness jumps from scene to scene and from reel to reel. On Android, set the system brightness low, give your video apps a darker per-app level in Screen & Notification Dimmer, and turn on Auto-Dim so a bright scene or white reel is dimmed as it appears. Keep the filter on Black so colours stay true.

Why video is different¶

Reading apps have fairly steady brightness. Video does not: a dark scene followed by a snowy one, or a black reel followed by one filmed on a white wall. A level that is fine for one is far too bright for the next.

Setup for video apps¶

  1. Per-app level: set YouTube, Instagram and your video player to around 60–70. Everything else stays at your usual level.
  2. Auto-Dim on, with Reaction speed on Medium. Fast can feel jumpy in films; Gentle may react too late for reels.
  3. Colour filter: Black. Tints change skin tones and the look of a film.
  4. Edge slider for quick fixes mid-film without pausing. How.

Streaming apps and Auto-Dim

Some streaming apps protect their video from screenshots. Auto-Dim measures brightness through Android's screenshot API, so it may not react inside those apps. Use a fixed per-app level for them instead.

Full-screen video and the status bar¶

In full-screen video the status bar is hidden anyway, but swiping down for a notification mid-film shows the bright shade. Dim notification bar covers that.

Frequently asked questions¶

How do I stop bright videos hurting my eyes at night?¶

Lower the system brightness, use a darker per-app level for video apps, and turn on Auto-Dim so bright scenes are dimmed as they appear.

Can I dim YouTube more than other apps?¶

Yes. Per-app profiles in Screen & Notification Dimmer let YouTube have its own darker level.
